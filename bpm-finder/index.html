Why the number matters

BPM for DJs, producers, and musicians

DJs use BPM to choose compatible transitions, set beat grids, and judge how much time-stretching a mix will need. Two tracks do not need identical tempos, but knowing the exact difference helps you decide whether to pitch one track, create a tempo change, or use a transition that does not depend on locked beats.

Producers use BPM to fit loops to a session, calculate delay times, program sidechain timing, and organize sample libraries. A loop labeled 100 BPM but actually recorded at 98 BPM will drift across a longer arrangement. Measuring the source before warping it saves corrections later.

For unreleased songs and client stems, local analysis is more than a convenience: your audio never leaves your device.

Musicians and dancers use tempo to set a metronome or practice a passage at controlled speeds. Video editors use the same value to place cuts, motion accents, and transitions on a repeatable pulse. BPM is simple data, but it connects timing decisions across an entire production.

Automatic analysis is strongest when the rhythm is steady. Live recordings with rubato, ambient pieces without clear transients, tempo maps, and unusual meters can produce a low-confidence answer. In those cases, isolate a representative section or use Tap Tempo while listening.

Read the result correctly

Half-time, double-time, and common ranges

A detector may report 70 BPM for a groove you count at 140. Both can describe the same spacing at different metrical levels.

RangeCommon feelWhat to check
60–80 BPMSlow, relaxed, or half-timeCould also be counted at 120–160
80–115 BPMMid-tempo pop, hip-hop, funkListen for the main backbeat
115–135 BPMUpbeat pop, house, danceUsually a clear four-on-the-floor pulse
135–175 BPMFast dance, drum and bass, double-timeCould feel slower at half the number

How can I find the BPM of an MP3?+

Open the detector, choose your MP3, and wait for local browser analysis. You will receive an estimate, confidence level, and alternate tempo interpretations.

Can I analyze an unreleased song privately?+

Yes. The audio is processed in your browser and is not uploaded to a server.

Should I trust automatic BPM or tap tempo?+

Use automatic detection first, then tap along when the track has loose timing, tempo changes, sparse percussion, or an ambiguous half-time groove.
